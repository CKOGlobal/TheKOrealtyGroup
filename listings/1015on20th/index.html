If you've been looking on the island

You already know the questions.

Windstorm, flood, rental rules, historic review, parking. Every house on Galveston Island comes with the same list, and most listings make you ask. Here it is up front — the house first, the island below.

"Which bedroom is the primary?"
The smallest one, and it is upstairs. The primary measures 16'1" × 10'5", about 167 sq ft. The other two are 15'10" × 13'5" and 15'10" × 13'6", about 212 and 214. All three are on the second floor, along with both full baths. If you need a large first-floor suite, this is not that house. If you need three rooms nobody has to fight over — parents and adult kids, two couples splitting a beach house, roommates, or a rental where every bedroom books at the same rate — it is.
"Where are the bathrooms?"
Two full baths upstairs, a powder room down. The full baths measure 6'9" × 10'5" and 6'11" × 9'2". The powder room (2'8" × 5'2") sits off the entry hall, so guests never climb the stairs.
"What's on the first floor?"
An entry hall, living room, dining room, kitchen and powder room. The entry hall is 24'9" long. The living room (15'9" × 13'4") and dining room (15'9" × 13'3") share the same width and open to each other through a cased opening. The kitchen is 23'4" long and 10'5" wide, with an island, a gas range and a separate pantry.
"Does it work as a rental?"
Whether it may be rented short-term is a city question; see below. Whether the floor plan works as one is answered above: three bedrooms of near-equal size, two full baths on the same floor as the bedrooms, and a kitchen that seats people at the island.
"How big is it?"
The recorded square footage is being confirmed (pending). The eleven labeled rooms on the floor plan add up to one number; the appraisal district reports another; the MLS may show a third. None of them is wrong, and none of them is quoted here until the recorded figure is in hand. Why the numbers never match.

Galveston Island

The island questions, answered.

This is not a suburban house and you are not a commuter. Whether you are buying a second home, a rental, or a place to live year-round, these six items decide whether the deal closes. They are answered here in the order they kill contracts.

1. "What about windstorm insurance?"
On Galveston Island, windstorm is a separate policy from flood, usually written through the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association (TWIA), and a missing windstorm certificate is the most common reason an island contract falls apart. Whether this house holds a current WPI-8 Certificate of Compliance: being confirmed. No premium is quoted here; get your own quote from your own agent before you write an offer.
2. "What flood zone is it in?"
A flood zone is a parcel-level fact, not a city-wide one. Nothing here is assumed from the neighborhood. FEMA zone for this parcel: pending. Map panel and effective date: pending. Elevation certificate on file: pending. What Zone X, X500 and AE actually mean in Galveston County.
3. "Can I rent it short-term?"
The City of Galveston has required short-term rentals to register since 2015 (Ordinance 15-012), and collects hotel occupancy tax on them. Whether it is permitted at this address, and exactly what registration requires for this parcel, is being confirmed with the city directly rather than assumed: pending.
4. "Is it in a historic district?"
20th Street near Broadway sits close to the boundaries of the East End and Lost Bayou historic districts, where any change to the exterior goes before the city's Landmark Commission. Whether this parcel falls inside a district, and which one, is being verified against the City of Galveston's official district map: pending. If it does, exterior work needs Landmark Commission approval; interiors are not reviewed.
