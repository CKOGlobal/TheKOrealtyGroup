Before you have to ask

You already know the questions.

Bayou frontage, a well, acreage, a house first built in 2000. Here is what each of those actually means on this property.

"It's on the bayou. It's going to flood."

It is in FEMA Zone AE, and flood insurance is required for a financed purchase. That is stated plainly in The Details below, not in fine print.

What the map doesn't tell you is the floor. The finished floor is 16.20 feet, two feet above the 14.2-foot base flood elevation, per a finished-construction elevation certificate sealed in October 2024. That certificate is the document your insurance agent will want. Why it matters →

"Well and septic. So, country plumbing."

Private well with a softener, and reverse osmosis at the kitchen, the coffee station and the ice makers. The septic system has a dedicated pump serving the addition.

It isn't municipal, and you should know what to ask for when it isn't. What to ask for at the table →

"Acreage means an ag exemption, and a rollback bill waiting for whoever buys it."

Not here. The land carries no agricultural valuation on the 2026 appraisal roll, so there is no rollback exposure on a change of use.

Why that matters on any tract →

"Seven acres behind the trees means satellite internet."

Xfinity high-speed internet, with a dedicated router in the detached office.

"Built in 2000 is built in 2000."

It was taken to the studs and rebuilt in 2024. Steel beam foundation, 2x6 structural framing, plywood sheathing behind the interior walls, a metal roof in 2024, and the wrap-around porches retiled in 2026.

"I'll budget for what the seller pays in taxes."

Please don't. The current county assessed value is well below the list price, and taxes will likely reassess materially higher after a sale. Budget on market value, not the current bill.
