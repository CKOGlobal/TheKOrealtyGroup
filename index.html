How I work

Four things you can hold me to.

Not a philosophy. Just how the file gets handled from the first conversation to the day you get the keys.

01

I try to answer the questions before you ask them.

Most of the time you don't know what you don't know — that's the actual problem. I get ahead of everything I can see coming, so if it matters at closing you hear about it in week one. When something isn't on my radar, ask me. No question here is too small or too obvious.

02

I read the data, then design the plan around it.

Price, days on market, what actually sold two streets over. The strategy comes out of the numbers. It does not come out of a feeling about the market.

03

I'm a problem solver, not a problem creator.

Every transaction develops one. Mine get solved quietly, before they reach you as a crisis — because I speak title, mortgage and real estate fluently enough to fix it at the source.

04

The solution is a win for everyone.

A deal held together by one side losing does not close. I negotiate hard for you and I keep the other side of the table whole, because that is what gets you to the closing table at all.

Answered before you ask

The questions nobody wants to answer straight.

Here they are, up front. If your agent won't put these in writing before you hire them, that tells you something.

"How do I know you priced it right and not just high enough to win the listing?"
You get the data I used, not just the number. Comparable sales, current competition, days on market, and the price band where buyers in your neighborhood are actually financing. If you disagree with the number, you can argue with the evidence instead of with me.
"What happens when the appraisal comes in low?"
We plan for it before it happens. I speak mortgage and title fluently, which means I know what an underwriter will accept, what a reconsideration of value actually requires, and what the contract already allows us to do. This is the moment most deals die. It is also the moment preparation is worth the most.
"I live out of state. Do I have to fly in to sign?"
No. I am a Texas-commissioned remote online notary. Texas law requires the notary to be in Texas — it does not require you to be. You can sign from your kitchen in another state. Here is how that works.
"What is this actually going to cost me at closing?"
You get an estimate early, and it gets updated as the numbers firm up. Title policy, survey, taxes prorated, HOA transfer, lender fees. Nobody should learn what they are bringing to closing from the settlement statement.
"Is this house zoned to the schools I think it is?"
League City is served by two school districts, not one. Most of the city is Clear Creek ISD, but part of it is Dickinson ISD — and the line does not follow neighborhood names or subdivision marketing. I verify the attendance zone for your specific address with the district before you write an offer, because "it's in Clear Creek" is the single most expensive assumption a buyer can make here.
"Are you going to disappear once I sign the listing agreement?"
You have my cell. 281-650-7592. Not a team inbox, not a transaction coordinator you have never met. The person who took the listing is the person who works it.
